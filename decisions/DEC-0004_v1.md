# DEC-0004

## Decision

Human erasure in CCC is a human-only operation, not a constitutional right.

---

## Date

2026-10-09

---

## Context

The README described erasure as "the human's right to erase." Constitution v4.0 (Developed Candidate, not ratified; wking53214/constitution) contains no human right of erasure. Its only erasure-related provisions limit erasure: Article XVIII says a system shall not erase negative evidence merely because it conflicts with a preferred conclusion. Article XIX (Reversibility) prefers reversible changes and says irreversible actions require heightened scrutiny. CCC cannot restore an erased record today, so erasure is irreversible in the current code.

The erase step keeps the record and marks it ERASED. Anything that depended on it loses evidence status.

The decision was made by William N. King on 2026-10-09.

---

## Options Considered

A.

Keep the README wording and add a note that v4.0 does not ground erasure. Nothing else changes.

B.

Keep the behavior, change "right" to "human-only operation" in the README, and record the item as unratified mechanism.

C.

Remove the erasure operation until v4.0 or a ratified amendment supports it.

---

## Selected Option

B. The word "right" claims more than v4.0 supports. The behavior already matches the human-only, soft-erase design that Articles XVIII and XIX point toward, so only the description needs to change.

---

## Reason Selected

B fixes the unsupported claim without changing what the system does. Keeping the record and marking it ERASED fits Article XVIII better than deleting it. Keeping erasure human-only matches the caution Article XIX asks for with irreversible actions.

---

## Alternatives Rejected

A. It leaves an unsupported word in the README and a note that a reader can easily miss.

C. It breaks the dependency-aware erasure rule (DEC-0002, Article I.G) and several connector and constitution tests, and it is costly to reverse.

---

## Alternatives Deferred

A restore path for erased records. Article XIX suggests one, but it changes behavior, so it needs its own decision record.

---

## Assumptions

- The v4.0 text read on 2026-10-09 is the version that matters, and it is not ratified.
- No outside consumer relies on the word "right" as a guarantee.

If one of these turns out false, revisit the decision.

---

## Expected Impact

- The README describes erasure as a human-only operation, not a right.
- No code, behavior, or test changes.
- The restore gap stays open and is tracked as a deferred question.
